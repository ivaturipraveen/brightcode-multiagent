A plain-language guide to federal changes affecting SNAP food benefits, Medicaid (Apple Health), and work requirements — with specific information for Washington State's Vietnamese, Chinese, Somali, Filipino, and Latino communities. Isang simpleng gabay sa mga pagbabago sa federal na nakakaapekto sa mga benepisyo ng pagkain ng SNAP, Medicaid (Apple Health), at mga kinakailangan sa trabaho — na may partikular na impormasyon para sa mga komunidad ng Washington State. Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about H.R. 1 and your benefits

▼
Will I lose my SNAP benefits right away?
+
No. SNAP work requirements begin June 1, 2026. If you are a refugee or asylee, DSHS will automatically move you to state-funded benefits in May 2026 — same amount, no reapplication needed. You will not lose benefits before your scheduled eligibility review and screening.
I'm on DACA. Will I lose my health coverage?
+
Under H.R. 1, DACA recipients lose federal Medicaid eligibility. However, Washington State may provide state-funded alternatives. Contact the Washington Health Benefit Exchange at wahbexchange.org or call 1-855-923-4633 before your coverage ends. Do not wait until you receive a termination notice — act early.
My child is a U.S. citizen but I'm undocumented. Can my child still get benefits?
+
Yes. Your U.S. citizen child is fully eligible for SNAP and Medicaid benefits regardless of your status. If one person in a household becomes ineligible, other household members — including U.S. citizen children — can still receive their full benefits. Do not let fear stop you from applying for your children.
I received a notice about SNAP changes. Do I need to reapply?
+
For refugees and asylees shifting to state-funded SNAP in May 2026: No reapplication is needed. DSHS automatically transitions you. Your benefit amount stays exactly the same. Keep your contact information updated with DSHS so you receive all notices. If you have questions, call 877-501-2233.
What if I need help but don't speak English well?
+
DSHS provides free translation and interpreter services. Call 877-501-2233 and ask for an interpreter in your language. The Northwest Justice Project also provides legal help in multiple languages. REWA serves refugee and immigrant communities with dedicated language support. ICHS (International Community Health Services) serves patients in 70+ languages.
